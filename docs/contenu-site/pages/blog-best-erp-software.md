# The 20 Best ERP Software Systems in 2026, Compared

![Inovexa Team](https://inovexa-erp.com/logo.png) Inovexa Team

· October 15, 2026 · 14 min read

There is no single best ERP software for every company. What you can do is narrow the field quickly. This guide compares 20 ERP systems that buyers actually shortlist in 2026, grouped by the size of company they suit, with what each does well, where it falls short, how it is deployed and how it is priced.

We sell an ERP ourselves (Inovexa), so we have included it in the list and been as plain about its limits as about everyone else's. It is not at the top. For most of the products below, pricing is quote-based, so where we mention a number it is a published list price and should be checked with the vendor before you budget.

## How we grouped the best ERP software

Company size is the most useful first filter. We used three groups:

- **Enterprise:** large, often global companies with thousands of users, complex supply chains or heavy regulatory reporting. Projects usually run 12 months or longer with a systems integrator.
- **Mid-market:** companies from roughly 50 to 1,000 employees that have outgrown entry-level accounting software and need inventory, multi-entity finance or manufacturing.
- **SMB and open source:** smaller businesses, or teams with in-house technical skills that want low licence costs and control over the code.

## Enterprise ERP systems

### 1. SAP S/4HANA

**Best for:** large manufacturers, retailers and multinationals with complex operations across many countries.

S/4HANA covers almost every process a large company runs, with deep industry versions for areas such as oil and gas, automotive, utilities and consumer goods. The pool of SAP consultants worldwide is the largest of any ERP.

**Limitations:** projects are long and expensive, and you will depend on an integration partner. Many current customers are moving from the older SAP ECC because mainstream maintenance for ECC ends in 2027, which keeps experienced consultants busy and costly.

**Deployment:** cloud (sold through RISE with SAP), private cloud or on-premise. **Pricing:** quote-based subscription or licence.

### 2. Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP

**Best for:** large and upper mid-market companies that want a cloud-only finance and procurement platform.

Oracle's cloud suite is strong in financials, procurement, project accounting and enterprise performance management. It is delivered as true SaaS, with Oracle shipping updates every quarter, so customers stay on one version instead of planning big upgrade projects.

**Limitations:** quarterly updates mean regular regression testing. Licensing across the wider Oracle catalogue can be hard to read, and manufacturing depth is less of a reason to choose it than finance.

**Deployment:** Oracle-hosted cloud. **Pricing:** quote-based subscription.

### 3. Microsoft Dynamics 365 Finance and Supply Chain Management

**Best for:** large and upper mid-market manufacturers, distributors and retailers already using Microsoft 365 and Azure.

This is Microsoft's enterprise ERP, separate from Business Central. It handles multi-country finance, warehouse management, production planning and retail, and connects directly to Power BI, Power Automate and Teams. Microsoft's partner network is very large, so finding implementers in most countries is not hard.

**Limitations:** it is a large system that needs a capable partner, and Microsoft sets a minimum number of user licences. Costs climb as you add modules and add-on apps.

**Deployment:** Microsoft cloud. **Pricing:** Microsoft publishes per-user list prices; Finance has been listed at around $210 per user per month, with discounts for users who also take Supply Chain Management. Confirm current rates.

### 4. Infor CloudSuite

**Best for:** manufacturers and distributors in specific industries such as food and beverage, fashion, industrial equipment and automotive.

Infor sells industry-specific suites rather than one general product. CloudSuite Industrial (SyteLine), M3 and LN each target different types of manufacturing, and they come with a lot of sector functionality out of the box, which reduces custom development. The suites run on AWS.

**Limitations:** the product family is complicated to understand at first, and the partner ecosystem is smaller than SAP's or Microsoft's in many countries.

**Deployment:** multi-tenant cloud on AWS, with some on-premise options. **Pricing:** quote-based.

### 5. IFS Cloud

**Best for:** asset-heavy and project-based businesses: aerospace and defence, energy, construction, field service and equipment makers.

IFS combines ERP with enterprise asset management and field service management on one platform. If your revenue depends on maintaining equipment, running service contracts or delivering long projects, it covers that better than most general ERPs. IFS releases updates twice a year on a single codebase.

**Limitations:** it is more than a simple distributor or service business needs, and it is less widely known among smaller implementation partners.

**Deployment:** cloud or on-premise. **Pricing:** quote-based.

### 6. Workday Financial Management

**Best for:** services-heavy organisations, financial services, higher education and companies that already use Workday HCM.

Workday's strength is having finance and HR on the same data model. Headcount, payroll costs and financial planning line up without integration work, and reporting is fast.

**Limitations:** it is not built for manufacturing or complex inventory, so product companies usually look elsewhere. It sits at the top end on price.

**Deployment:** Workday cloud. **Pricing:** quote-based subscription.

## Mid-market ERP systems

### 7. Oracle NetSuite

**Best for:** fast-growing companies, especially SaaS, wholesale, e-commerce and services, that expect to add subsidiaries in other countries.

NetSuite was one of the first cloud ERPs and remains the default mid-market shortlist name. NetSuite OneWorld handles multiple subsidiaries, currencies and consolidations well, and the SuiteCloud platform allows deep customisation.

**Limitations:** costs grow with each module, user and edition, and renewal price increases are a common complaint. Serious customisation needs SuiteScript developers. We cover these costs in more detail in [our guide to hidden ERP costs](https://inovexa-erp.com/blog-erp-hidden-costs.html).

**Deployment:** Oracle cloud. **Pricing:** quote-based, usually an annual base licence plus per-user fees and modules.

### 8. Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central

**Best for:** small and mid-sized companies that live in Microsoft 365.

Business Central covers finance, sales, purchasing, inventory, projects and basic manufacturing. It works closely with Outlook, Excel and Teams, and the AppSource marketplace has thousands of add-ons for specific industries and countries. Partners exist almost everywhere.

**Limitations:** complex manufacturing and very high transaction volumes push it to its limits. Much of the real value comes from partner add-ons, each with its own fee.

**Deployment:** Microsoft cloud, or on-premise through a partner. **Pricing:** Microsoft publishes list prices; Essentials has been around $80 per user per month and Premium around $110. Implementation is extra.

### 9. Acumatica

**Best for:** mid-sized distributors, manufacturers and construction firms that want many users without per-seat fees.

Acumatica prices by the resources and transaction volume you use rather than by named users, so you can give access to occasional users without a licence bill. It has industry editions for distribution, manufacturing, construction and retail-commerce, and a modern browser interface.

**Limitations:** its partner network and customer base are concentrated in North America, so check local support if you operate elsewhere.

**Deployment:** cloud (SaaS or private cloud) or on-premise. **Pricing:** quote-based, by resource level and modules.

### 10. Sage Intacct

**Best for:** finance teams in services, non-profits, SaaS and healthcare that need strong multi-entity accounting.

Intacct is a cloud financial management system, and a good one. Its dimensional chart of accounts lets you report by department, location, project or grant without a huge account list, and multi-entity consolidation is fast.

**Limitations:** it is mainly a finance product. If you need manufacturing, warehouse management or deep inventory, you will be integrating other systems.

**Deployment:** cloud. **Pricing:** quote-based.

### 11. Epicor Kinetic

**Best for:** discrete and make-to-order manufacturers.

Kinetic has detailed production planning, scheduling, quality and shop-floor control, built for manufacturers that handle many product variants or jobs.

**Limitations:** service and distribution-only businesses rarely need its manufacturing depth and will find the project scope heavy.

**Deployment:** cloud or on-premise. **Pricing:** quote-based.

### 12. SAP Business One

**Best for:** small and mid-sized manufacturers and distributors, including subsidiaries of groups that run SAP at head office.

Business One is SAP's product for smaller companies. It covers finance, sales, purchasing, inventory and production, and is sold and supported by a large worldwide partner network. It can run on SAP HANA or Microsoft SQL Server.

**Limitations:** the experience depends heavily on your partner. Many industry needs are met through partner add-ons, and it is a different product from S/4HANA, so moving up later is a new implementation.

**Deployment:** on-premise or partner-hosted cloud. **Pricing:** quote-based through partners, as licence or subscription.

### 13. Sage X3

**Best for:** mid-sized manufacturers and distributors with several sites or countries, including process industries such as food and chemicals.

Sage X3 handles multi-site, multi-company and multi-currency operations, with traceability, lot control and process manufacturing features that matter in regulated sectors.

**Limitations:** results depend a lot on partner quality, and cloud options are less mature than those of products built for the cloud from the start.

**Deployment:** on-premise or hosted cloud. **Pricing:** quote-based through partners.

### 14. Unit4 ERPx

**Best for:** people-based organisations: professional services, public sector, non-profits and higher education.

Unit4 is built around people and projects rather than products. Time, expenses, resourcing, project accounting and grant management are central to the design.

**Limitations:** it is not a fit for companies with manufacturing or complex inventory.

**Deployment:** cloud. **Pricing:** quote-based.

### 15. Deltek

**Best for:** project-based firms: architecture and engineering, consultancies, agencies and government contractors.

Deltek has several products for different project businesses. Costpoint is widely used by US government contractors that need compliant cost accounting. Vantagepoint targets architecture and engineering firms, and Maconomy serves agencies and consultancies.

**Limitations:** each product is specialised, and Costpoint in particular is built around US government contracting rules.

**Deployment:** cloud or on-premise, depending on the product. **Pricing:** quote-based.

## SMB and open-source ERP

### 16. Odoo

**Best for:** small and mid-sized businesses that want to start with a few apps and add more over time.

Odoo, from Belgium, has a long list of apps covering accounting, inventory, manufacturing, CRM, e-commerce, HR and more, all on one database. The Community edition is open source and free. Enterprise adds features, hosting and support, with per-user pricing that varies by country and plan.

**Limitations:** heavy customisation can make major version upgrades painful. Some important features, including the full accounting app, are Enterprise only, and partner quality varies widely.

**Deployment:** Odoo Online, Odoo.sh or self-hosted. **Pricing:** Community free; Enterprise per user per month.

### 17. ERPNext

**Best for:** cost-conscious manufacturers, distributors and service firms with some in-house technical skill.

ERPNext is fully open source (GPL), built by Frappe on its own framework. It covers accounting, inventory, manufacturing, projects, HR and CRM, and all features are available without an enterprise edition.

**Limitations:** the partner network is smaller than the commercial vendors', and self-hosting means you are responsible for upgrades, backups and security.

**Deployment:** self-hosted or Frappe Cloud. **Pricing:** software free; hosting and support paid.

### 18. Zoho (Books, Inventory, Zoho One)

**Best for:** small businesses that want low-cost accounting, inventory and CRM from one vendor.

Zoho is not a single ERP, but its apps (Books, Inventory, CRM, People and others) connect well, and Zoho One bundles more than 40 of them. Pricing is published and low compared with most systems on this list.

**Limitations:** manufacturing is basic, and because these are separate apps, complex multi-entity or production scenarios outgrow it.

**Deployment:** Zoho cloud. **Pricing:** published; Books has a free tier in many regions and paid plans per organisation per month.

### 19. Sage 200

**Best for:** small and mid-sized businesses in the UK and Ireland.

Sage 200 covers finance, commercial, stock and project accounting, and a large number of UK accountants and resellers already know it.

**Limitations:** it is focused on the UK and Ireland, and parts of the architecture are older than cloud-native alternatives.

**Deployment:** on-premise or partner-hosted. **Pricing:** quote-based through partners.

### 20. Inovexa

**Best for:** growing SMEs and mid-sized companies operating in several countries.

Inovexa is a cloud ERP built in Tunis, Tunisia, and part of MSAI Systems. It has 11 modules (Dashboard, HR, Finance, Supply Chain, Production, Purchases, Sales & CRM, Logistics, AI Engine, Reports and Analytics) on one database. You pay for the modules you deploy, so a trading company can start with Finance, Purchases and Supply Chain and add Production or HR later. It handles multiple currencies and legal entities, runs in English and French, and is used by more than 340 companies in over 12 countries. A standard setup typically takes around 48 hours. See [our pricing page](https://inovexa-erp.com/pricing.html) for plans.

**Limitations:** Inovexa is a younger vendor with a smaller partner ecosystem than SAP, Microsoft or Oracle. It is not designed for very large enterprises with thousands of users and deep industry-specific requirements, where the products at the top of this list are a better fit.

**Deployment:** cloud. **Pricing:** modular, published on our pricing page.

## How to choose the right ERP software

These criteria will remove most of the wrong options. For a fuller process, see [our step-by-step guide to choosing an ERP](https://inovexa-erp.com/blog-how-to-choose-erp.html).

### Start with your core process, not the feature list

Write down the process that makes you money: make-to-order production, project delivery, importing and reselling, subscription billing. Then shortlist systems built around that process. A project firm should look at Unit4 or Deltek before a manufacturing system, and a job-shop manufacturer should look at Epicor before Workday.

### Count entities, currencies and countries

If you invoice in more than one currency or run more than one legal entity, test consolidation and intercompany transactions in the demo with your own data. This is where many entry-level systems break. Our article on [multi-currency ERP](https://inovexa-erp.com/blog-multi-currency-erp.html) lists what to check.

### Check local tax and e-invoicing support

Ask each vendor which countries they support natively for VAT, payroll and e-invoicing, and which depend on a partner add-on. A missing localisation is expensive to build.

### Work out the three-year cost

Add licences, implementation, data migration, training, add-ons and internal staff time over three years. Implementation often costs as much as or more than the first years of licences on the larger systems.

### Look at the partner, not only the product

For SAP, Microsoft, Sage and Odoo, the partner does most of the work. Ask for two references of similar size and industry, and speak to them without the partner present.

### Be realistic about time to go live

Enterprise projects commonly take a year or more; mid-market projects often take three to nine months. If you need to be live by a fixed date, such as a new financial year, tell vendors upfront and ask what they would cut. Our [ERP implementation guide](https://inovexa-erp.com/blog-erp-implementation-guide.html) covers typical timelines.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best ERP software in 2026?

There is no single best ERP for every company. For large multinationals, SAP S/4HANA, Oracle Fusion Cloud ERP and Microsoft Dynamics 365 Finance and Supply Chain Management are the usual shortlist. For mid-sized companies, NetSuite, Dynamics 365 Business Central and Acumatica are common choices. Smaller businesses often look at Odoo, ERPNext or Zoho. The right choice depends on your core process, number of entities and countries, and budget.

### How much does ERP software cost?

Most enterprise and mid-market ERP vendors use quote-based pricing. Some publish list prices: Microsoft lists Dynamics 365 Business Central Essentials at around $80 per user per month, for example. Implementation, data migration, training and add-ons often add as much as or more than the licence cost, so compare the total cost over three years rather than the monthly fee.

### What is the best ERP for small businesses?

Small businesses usually do well with Dynamics 365 Business Central, Odoo, Zoho, ERPNext or SAP Business One, depending on whether they need manufacturing, how much technical skill they have in-house and which software they already use. If you trade in several countries or currencies, check multi-currency and multi-entity support early.

### Is open-source ERP a good option?

Open-source ERP such as Odoo Community and ERPNext can keep licence costs low and give you control over the code. The trade-off is that you or a partner are responsible for hosting, upgrades, security and support. It suits companies with some in-house technical capacity or a reliable local partner.

### How long does an ERP implementation take?

Enterprise ERP projects commonly take a year or more. Mid-market projects often take three to nine months. Smaller cloud ERP setups with standard processes can be live in days or weeks. Data quality, customisation and how many sites go live at once have the biggest effect on the timeline.

### Cloud ERP or on-premise ERP: which should I choose?

Most new ERP projects in 2026 are cloud-based, because the vendor handles hosting, updates and security, and costs are spread over a subscription. On-premise still makes sense where regulation, data residency rules or existing infrastructure require it. Several vendors on this list, such as Epicor, IFS and Acumatica, offer both.
